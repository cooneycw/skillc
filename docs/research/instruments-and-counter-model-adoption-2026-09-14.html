Section 01Vocabulary: spec, instrument, harness

Recommendation

Keep "spec." Do not rename it to "harness." Adopt "harness" for the system, which is currently unnamed. And promote "instrument" - the word that is actually missing - from a line in your global directive to a first-class term in both repos' documentation.

The instinct to rename is worth taking seriously and then declining, for three reasons.

It is a category error. A spec names an artifact; a harness names a system. In Böckeler's split, a spec is one feedforward control sitting inside the harness alongside skills, context files and constitutions - and the harness also contains the feedback controls a spec has nothing to do with: linters, tests, CI, sandboxes, gates. Rename spec to harness and you have no word left for the document.

It has no red case. A repo-wide rename cannot report the other verdict. There is no input that makes it say "this was a mistake." By your own Negative Control directive that is a change you are not entitled to make at scale, and the discipline should apply to its own adoption first.

The missing word is not "harness." Your directive already contains the term that does the work - instrument: "anything whose output is read as evidence." It appears nowhere in either repo's documentation vocabulary. It is the noun the negative-control rule attaches to, the noun #924 needs in order to scope itself, and the noun that makes the bounding rule statable.

TermNamesAction
specthe durable statement of intent for a piece of workkeep unchanged - /spec:*, .specify/, the tiering in spec/help.md all stay
instrumentanything whose output is read as evidence - a gate, a check, a probe, a preflight, a report, a monitor, a testpromote from directive prose to a defined term in docs/agents/, with the bounding rule attached
harnessthe system of controls around the model: context, tools, permissions, sandboxes, gates, feedback loopsadopt for the system only - never as a synonym for spec or for any single artifact
counter-modelthe model that did not implement the change under reviewadopt - stated as a property, not a tool name, so a Qwen or Gemma lane inherits it without an edit
The one thing to watch

"Harness" is already load-bearing in CPP for something else: lib/cpp_memory/harness.py and scripts/install-memory-harness.sh use it to mean which agent CLI is running (the friction ledger's harness tag, #557/#562). That is a narrower and older meaning than the systems sense. Introducing the broad sense without noting the collision would produce exactly the ambiguity this section exists to prevent. The definition entry should say so explicitly.

Two new CPP ADRs, each produced by an issue rather than tracked as its own

ADR 0007

The counter-model is the model that did not implement

Records: the reviewing model must not be the implementing model; the stage is default and tier-bound rather than a separate opt-in command; the accept/reject ratio is recorded because the primary model judging the adversary is the failure mode; and the stage does not ship without its own red case. Produced by CPP #934. Supersedes nothing; related to the /flow:auto_codex design it retires.

ADR 0008

What an instrument is, and when it needs a negative control

Records the bounding rule: an instrument needs a committed negative control when its verdict is consumed by a decision that will not independently re-derive the fact. With the carve-out that makes it affordable - a unit test whose failure the surrounding suite would catch is not individually load-bearing - and the escalation that makes it strict: a check whose green is read by a different session or a different repo always needs one. Produced by CPP #932.

Why the ADRs are not their own issues

An ADR is an artifact of a decision, not a unit of work. Your issue-quality assessment measured the cost of the alternative directly: "18 tracker entries for six shared outcomes." Each ADR here is an acceptance item on the issue that makes the decision, so the tracker carries five work items across CPP rather than five plus five documents.

The three repos keep three different conventions - leave them

CPP uses docs/decisions/NNNN-*.md. Kyle uses docs/adr/NNNN-*.md with a README. CxPP has neither. Unifying the paths is another rename with no red case; the only real gap is CxPP having nowhere to record a decision at all.

So: create docs/decisions/ in CxPP to match its upstream, seed it with the two ADRs marked as adopted from claude-power-pack, and stop there. That is a place to write decisions down, not an ADR practice with process attached.

Section 03The wave

Five stages. The ordering is not preference - each stage's evidence depends on the one before it, and two of the dependencies are hard.

Stage 1 · CPP · blocking

Define the instrument and bound the rule

CPP #932. Produces ADR 0008 and a narrowing edit to the Negative Control directive. Hard dependency: #924's machinery cannot scope itself without this. Today its scope is "gates," undefined, with seven consumers that are the seven that happened to fail recently - a list that narrows rather than a rule that generalises.

Stage 2 · CPP

Land #924's machinery against the gate that certifies the others

CPP #933 plus a comment re-pointing #924's first consumer. make negative-fixture-check prints ok - every constructed absence asserts its precondition while its own docstring says it detects only statically-visible PATH replacement. An absence built by chmod, by a nonzero stub, or by a monkeypatched import is invisible and reports the same green. It is the instrument currently certifying that your other instruments are honest, and it overstates its scope.

Stage 3 · CPP

Make the counter-model routine, and give it a red case

CPP #934. Produces ADR 0007. Promotes the Codex stage from /flow:auto_codex into /flow:auto bound to tier, retires the separate command, adds the stage's own negative control, and records the accept/reject ratio. Evidence: 0 of the last 200 merged CPP PRs carry the Codex review marker, against a positive control of 199/200 for a marker known to be present.

Stage 4 · CxPP · after 1 and 3

Adopt the definition; make the reciprocal routine

CxPP #240 and #241. CxPP already ships claude-code-review with the ownership boundary drawn correctly - "Claude advises; Codex retains ownership of edits, tests, and lifecycle decisions" - but its trigger is escalation-on-stuck, and grep finds no reference to it in flow-auto.

Constraint already recorded in CxPP #228: claude-code-review is native to CxPP, so no CPP edit reaches it. The reciprocal half must be built there, not generated. That is the one piece of this wave that cannot flow through the source bridge.

Stage 5 · Kyle · after 1

Carry the methodology across the container boundary

Kyle #1209 and #1210, plus a comment on #1176. See Section 04 - the finding here is larger than skill retention.

Section 04Kyle, specifically

You asked whether Kyle inherits only a subset of CPP's skills, and if so whether the new methodologies are in the retained set. I read core/session_backends.py. The answer has three parts and the third one is the important one.

all
CPP command namespaces reach containers - no subset
yes
codex runs inside a container (#1001, closed)
no
the governing directive reaches a container (#1176, open)

1 · Commands are not subsetted; skills are

_host_dir_entry_mounts(f"{home}/commands", …) is called with no exclude, so every entry in ~/.claude/commands is mounted per-entry with each symlink resolved by realpath - the design that exists because a directory bind-mount would deliver 52 dangling links into a different repository. The whole CPP slash-command surface reaches a container. Only skills takes an exclude_skills tuple. So a counter-model stage living in /flow:auto arrives in containers automatically; nothing needs to be added to a retained list.

2 · The counter-model can run there - my prior assumption was wrong

I had recorded that Kyle containers are Claude-only workers with codex and qwen host-side. That is superseded. Kyle #1001 (closed) mounts the delegation CLIs: the codex binary at /usr/local/bin/codex - a static-pie musl ELF with zero library dependencies, measured running unmodified in kyle-session:latest - and codex credentials as a directory mount at /run/kyle/codex, deliberately not a file, because of the inode pin and codex's 240-hour token cadence. The code notes that #808's "not provided, deliberately" described the orchestrator case and never described implementation_driver=codex; Kyle ADR 0004 supersedes it.

3 · The governing directive does not cross the boundary

Kyle #1176, open: ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md is not mounted into session containers. Containers mount exactly two paths from ~/.claude - projects/ and settings.json. CLAUDE.md sits one level above the mounted subdirectory, so nothing carries it. Container is now the default placement.

The issue was filed about the Nit Store rule. The Negative Control directive lives in the same file and has the same problem - and it is the directive this entire wave depends on. Every containerised session has been building instruments under a rule it could not read.

This is why "put the methodology in the retained skills" is not sufficient as a strategy: the commands arrive, the codex binary arrives, and the rule that says what to do with them does not. I filed a comment on #1176 rather than a duplicate issue, because the mount fix is the same fix - what changed is that its priority is no longer about one disposition mechanism.

Section 06Where I could be wrong

  • The bounding rule is still untested. I have not enumerated the instruments it captures in any of the three repos. If the real count is hundreds rather than tens, "affordable" was wrong and the rule needs another narrowing. Issue A's first acceptance item is the enumeration, precisely so the rule meets its denominator before anything is built on it.
  • I did not read all six CPP ADRs in full - I read 0006 closely and the others by title and scope. "No existing ADR needs revision" is a judgement from that, not from a line-by-line reading. ADR 0002's threat model is the one most likely to have an instrument claim buried in it.
  • The CxPP reciprocal may be harder than Stage 4 implies. #228 records that claude-code-review is native and no CPP edit reaches it, but I did not read its script or verify that a routine (rather than escalation) invocation is even supported by the Agent SDK path it uses. If the SDK call assumes a blocker argument, promoting it is a rewrite rather than a trigger change.
  • Kyle's container evidence is read from source, not from a running container. The mount list is what DOCKER_TOOL_MOUNTS declares - the recipe, not a built artifact. Kyle's own tests/container_surface.py makes exactly this distinction and notes that #836 made the recipe reach a running image. I did not exec into a container to confirm /usr/local/bin/codex is present in the image you are actually running.
  • Seven issues may be too few rather than too many. I optimised against the inflation your assessment measured. If the counter-model promotion turns out to need separate work for the tier binding, the ratio instrument and the red case, issue C is three issues wearing one number - which is the failure mode in the opposite direction, and the one that hides scope rather than inflating it.
